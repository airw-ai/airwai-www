FAA-ACCEPTED

You're cleared to modernize, and expected to.

Part 139 is performance-based: the daily self-inspection rule (14 CFR 139.327) sets what you must inspect and leaves the method to the airport, and the FAA's move to Safety Management Systems is steering airports toward exactly this kind of continuous, data-driven monitoring. The FAA does not certify inspection devices, it accepts your inspection program. LAIRA produces the geolocated, time-stamped, auditable record that rule asks for, a qualified person reviews and signs it, and it files into the same records an FAA certification inspector already reviews. You adopt it the way you have adopted every inspection method before it, with no waiver and no device certification to wait on.

The squeeze

The mandate grew. Your team didn't.

FAA Part 139 already asks for a daily self-inspection of the movement area. The new Safety Management System (SMS) requirement adds continuous hazard tracking on top. Most airports carry both by hand, understaffed. LAIRA drives the airfield once and returns geolocated, severity-scored condition plus a NOTAM-ready record, FAA-accepted, so the mandate stops competing with the rest of the day.
