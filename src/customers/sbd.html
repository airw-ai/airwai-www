The problem

The federal self-inspection SBD must run every day tops out at 20–40 observations per shift by hand.

Before LAIRA, SBD’s airfield inspection workflow looked like every other Part 139 airport’s: certified inspectors driving the airfield by hand, recording observations on paper logs, transcribing to spreadsheets at end of shift. Coverage was inconsistent across shifts and weather; early-stage defects routinely went undetected; data was fragmented across formats that didn’t roll up into a maintenance plan.

The constraint wasn’t the inspectors. The constraint was the medium: a single human observer captures 20–40 observations per shift, and every one is a record an FAA inspector can ask to see. The airfield generates orders of magnitude more condition data than a clipboard can hold.

Manual inspections produced only 20–40 observations per shift with inconsistent results, leaving defects undetected and fragmented across logs.

— Esri case study

The solution

LAIRA captures continuously. ArcGIS makes it actionable.

SBD runs LAIRA, the Layered Autonomous Inference and Reasoning Agent platform, inside the airport’s existing Esri ArcGIS stack. The determination engine decides what each field condition means and publishes the result as geo-tagged, citation-backed anomalies straight into the airport’s ArcGIS Online environment, captured on operations vehicles the airport already drives. ArcGIS Field Maps directs crews to prioritized repair locations. ArcGIS Dashboards give leadership real-time oversight across the airfield. The record the FAA requires builds itself as the airport works.

Our team has a consistent, objective baseline across shifts and conditions, strengthening our compliance posture without adding burden to staff.
